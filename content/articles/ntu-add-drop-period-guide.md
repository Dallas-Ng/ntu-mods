# NTU Add/Drop Period: How It Works and How to Use It

Add/drop is the window after STARS bidding closes and the semester begins, where students can still add or drop modules as seats reopen. Many students assume it is too late by then — in practice, it is often the best chance to get a module that was full during Round 1 and Round 2.

> **Quick summary:** Add/drop begins when the semester officially starts and typically runs for the first one to two weeks of teaching. Seats reopen as other students drop modules, especially in the first few days. Check exact dates on the [NTU Academic Calendar](/articles/ntu-academic-calendar-ay2026) for your semester — add/drop dates are set each academic year.

## When add/drop happens

Add/drop follows directly after STARS Round 2 closes and the semester begins. It is not a fixed-length window across all semesters — always confirm the exact start and end dates on the [NTU Academic Calendar](/articles/ntu-academic-calendar-ay2026), since these shift slightly each academic year.

Unlike STARS Round 1 and Round 2, which are scheduled bidding windows before teaching starts, add/drop happens while classes are already running. You are adjusting a timetable that is already partly locked in.

## Why seats open up during add/drop

Seats become available during add/drop for a few consistent reasons:

- **Students drop modules after attending the first class** and deciding the workload, content, or schedule does not work for them
- **Students who over-registered during STARS to hedge their options** drop the modules they end up not needing
- **Clashes surface** once a student's full timetable is confirmed, forcing drops on one side of the clash
- **Prerequisite or programme issues** get caught late, causing administrative drops that free the seat

Because of this, the very first few days of add/drop typically see the most movement. A module that showed zero seats at the end of Round 2 can reopen within the first day or two of add/drop as students finalise their real schedules.

## How to actually use add/drop

**Check daily, especially the first week.** Seats can appear and disappear within hours. Checking once is not enough — the [Module Browser](/mods) reflects live seat availability, so check the specific module and index you want each day.

**Check every index of the module you want**, not just the one you originally bid for. A module can have multiple indexes at different times — one index reopening does not mean all of them are full or all of them are open. See [NTU STARS: How the Module Registration System Works](/articles/ntu-stars-guide) for how indexes work across rounds.

**Have your backup shortlist ready.** If your first-choice module has not reopened by mid add/drop, do not wait indefinitely — check your backup options from your original planning shortlist. See [How to Plan NTU Modules Before STARS Opens](/articles/how-to-plan-for-modules) for building a shortlist with real backups.

**Re-verify against your degree audit.** A module reopening during add/drop does not confirm it still satisfies your programme's requirements — cross-check with your [degree audit](/articles/ntu-degree-audit-guide) before committing, especially for BDE or UE slots.

**Attend the class before deciding to drop something else.** If you are using add/drop to swap into a module, sit in on the first session if possible before dropping your current registration — module content and instructor style can differ from what you expected going in.

## What add/drop does not fix

Add/drop only affects registration availability. It does not change:

- **Prerequisite requirements** — a failed prerequisite check will still block registration during add/drop, the same as during STARS
- **Programme restrictions** — modules restricted to specific programmes remain restricted
- **AU limits** — NTU's per-semester AU cap still applies; you cannot exceed it just because it is add/drop
- **Whether a module counts toward graduation** — always confirm against your [degree audit](/articles/ntu-degree-audit-guide), not just STARS/add-drop availability

## Add/drop vs Round 1 vs Round 2

| | Round 1 | Round 2 | Add/drop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timing | Before semester starts | Shortly after Round 1 | After semester begins |
| Demand | Highest | Moderate | Variable, front-loaded |
| Best for | Popular/competitive modules | Backup options, dropped seats | Catching drops in real time |
| Seat movement | Bidding-driven | Bidding-driven | Student-driven, daily |

## Frequently asked questions

### When does NTU add/drop start?

For full-time undergraduates, NTU runs add/drop during Teaching Weeks 1 and 2 of each semester. For AY2026/2027, Semester 1 add/drop starts in the week of 10 August 2026 and Semester 2 in the week of 11 January 2027. BDE, UE and GERPE allocation results are released shortly before, in early August and early January. See [NTU BDE Allocation Results](/articles/ntu-bde-allocation-results-release-date) for how that fits together.

### Can I still get a popular module during add/drop?

Sometimes. Popular modules that were full through Round 1 and Round 2 do occasionally reopen as students drop them in the first week of classes. It is not guaranteed, but checking daily during the first week gives you the best chance.

### Is add/drop the same as dropping a module after the deadline?

No. Add/drop is the official window where registration changes are unrestricted. Dropping after add/drop officially ends typically requires going through your school and may result in a transcript notation — check official NTU policy for your situation.

### Does add/drop availability mean a module counts toward my degree?

No. Add/drop only reflects registration availability, the same as STARS. Always cross-check against your [degree audit](/articles/ntu-degree-audit-guide) to confirm a module satisfies your programme's requirements before relying on it.
